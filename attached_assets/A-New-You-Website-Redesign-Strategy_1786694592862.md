# A New You — Website Audit, Repositioning and AI Redesign Brief

**Website:** https://www.anewyou.com.au/  
**Prepared:** 14 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Client-facing strategic direction and an implementation brief for an AI website-building agent

---

## 1. Executive decision

The existing website does not primarily have a design problem. It has a **business-positioning and information-architecture problem**.

At present, A New You appears to be several different businesses sharing one website:

1. Personal transformation and wellness sessions
2. Leadership and business mentoring
3. Structured programs, including Luminous Break Free and Savvy Start Up
4. Australian Aromatic Essences and other “growth tools”
5. A reseller or business-opportunity offer
6. Hypnotherapy, NLP, Time-Line Therapy and weight-related support

All of these may be legitimate parts of Christine Long’s work, but they are presented with nearly equal importance. The visitor is therefore required to work out what A New You does, whether it is intended for them, which methodology matters, and what to do next. Most visitors will not do that work.

### Recommended strategic direction

Position A New You first as a **founder-led personal and professional transformation practice for established women, business owners and leaders who feel successful on paper but stuck, depleted or disconnected in practice**.

The central offer should be Christine’s ability to identify and help shift the deeper patterns behind stalled progress. Programs and aromatic products then become supporting pathways—not competing identities.

### Recommended positioning statement

> A New You helps established women, business owners and leaders move beyond the patterns keeping them stuck, so they can regain clarity, confidence and momentum in life and business.

### Recommended primary conversion

Every core page should lead toward one clear, low-friction action:

> **Book a complimentary 20-minute Clarity Call**

Secondary actions may be “Explore Ways to Work Together” and “Shop Australian Aromatic Essences,” but these must not compete visually with the primary call to action.

### Important commercial decision before copy is finalised

Christine must confirm which offer currently produces the best combination of:

- Revenue
- Client outcomes
- Personal enthusiasm
- Capacity to deliver
- Opportunity for growth

This brief assumes that **high-trust, high-value transformation and mentoring services** are the primary business, with programs and products supporting that work. If product sales or the reseller model is in fact the main commercial priority, the architecture should be adjusted before development.

## 2. What the current website communicates

The homepage currently presents Body Wisdom, Mind Wisdom, life-changing programs, business opportunities and global gatherings as parallel entry points. It then introduces Christine using broad concepts such as heart-centred leadership, awareness, sustainable success and “Igniting the Brilliance in YOU.” The navigation adds programs, multiple categories of essences, books, product information, reseller content, six About-related pages, insights, payments, contact and clinic information.

This reveals a large and diverse body of work, but not a simple buying proposition.

The most commercially useful material currently on the site is buried. In particular:

- Christine has founded A New You and has decades of experience.
- She works with entrepreneurs, wellness leaders and people seeking meaningful personal change.
- She offers in-person, telephone and Zoom consultations.
- She has named methodologies including Core Issue Elimination and Inner Insight Mastery.
- She provides personal, business and wellness pathways rather than a single generic coaching package.
- She has testimonials and a long professional history that can establish trust.

These strengths should lead the new website. They should not be hidden beneath jargon, long menus or multiple unexplained brands.

## 3. Principal findings from the audit

### 3.1 The website does not answer the five-second questions

A new visitor should be able to answer the following almost immediately:

1. What does this business do?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. Why should I trust this person?
5. What should I do next?

The current homepage does not answer these questions plainly. Phrases such as “Body Wisdom,” “Mind Wisdom,” “activate,” “illuminate,” “liberate,” “wealth wellness consciousness,” “power of attraction” and “Luminous You” require interpretation before they convey a practical benefit.

### 3.2 The organisation is inside-out rather than customer-led

The site is arranged around Christine’s concepts, methods and product taxonomy. Buyers generally arrive with problems, not with knowledge of a provider’s internal methodology.

Visitors are more likely to think:

- “I have achieved a lot, but I feel stuck.”
- “I keep repeating the same pattern.”
- “I have lost confidence or direction.”
- “My business is draining me.”
- “I know what to do, but I cannot seem to follow through.”

The redesigned site should begin with these recognisable states, then explain Christine’s approach.

### 3.3 There are too many equally weighted pathways

The current navigation contains approximately 30 visible links and sub-links before blog content is considered. Even the About section is fragmented across About Us, profile/resume, expertise, biography, qualifications and testimonials.

This creates:

- Decision fatigue
- Weak page hierarchy
- Repetitive content
- Unclear SEO targets
- A sense that the visitor must understand the whole system before enquiring

### 3.4 The language is abstract, repetitive and difficult to substantiate

There is a heavy concentration of capitalisation, ellipses, quotation marks, invented terminology and superlatives. Examples include “MIRACULOUS RESULTS,” “revolutionary,” “laser sharp accuracy in minutes,” “permanent change,” “quantum wellness tools,” and “ensures” particular outcomes.

This language may reflect Christine’s natural enthusiasm, but online it can reduce rather than increase credibility. Affluent, established clients generally respond better to specificity, restraint, evidence and calm confidence.

### 3.5 Trust material exists but is not organised as proof

Christine’s longevity, qualifications, author contribution, named methodologies, client feedback and range of delivery options should form a deliberate trust sequence. At present, facts and claims are mixed together across multiple pages.

The new site should separate:

- Verifiable credentials
- Experience and professional story
- The defined process
- Client testimonials
- Appropriate limitations and disclaimers

### 3.6 The website contains visible legacy and quality-control issues

Observed issues include:

- A footer copyright date of 2016
- Inconsistent naming: “Anew You,” “A New You,” “Luminous Living” and Australian Aromatic Essences
- Duplicated/overloaded menu structures
- A shopping cart visible even when no products are present
- Template remnants and placeholder text on at least one indexed page, including links to the original theme demonstration site
- Inconsistent URLs and page labels
- Excessive use of title case, capitals, symbols and unusual spacing
- Calls to action such as Contact, Booking, Payment and free download competing without an obvious funnel
- Old references such as Skype alongside current channels

These issues make the business feel less current and less professionally governed than Christine’s experience warrants.

### 3.7 Content and regulatory risk requires review

Claims relating to weight loss, addictions, pain, health outcomes, essences and “permanent” or “miraculous” change must be reviewed before migration. Avoid guarantees and claims that cannot be demonstrated with suitable evidence.

The TGA states that it regulates advertising of therapeutic goods, while health-service advertising may also fall under Australian Consumer Law and, where relevant, Ahpra requirements. If aromatic essences are presented with therapeutic claims, obtain appropriate regulatory/legal review and confirm product status, permitted indications and supporting evidence. See the [TGA guidance on advertising health services involving therapeutic goods](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/advertising-health-services-involve-therapeutic-goods).

This document is a marketing and website strategy, not legal advice.

## 5. Ideal client definition

### Primary audience

An established woman, founder, business owner, leader or wellness professional, typically 40+, who:

- Is capable, intelligent and accustomed to solving problems
- Has achieved meaningful results but feels stuck in one or more areas
- Is tired of surface-level advice and repeated short-term fixes
- Values discretion, personal attention and an experienced guide
- Is open to holistic or integrative ideas but still wants a clear, professional process
- Can invest in private support when trust and value are established
- Wants lasting, practical change rather than more information

### Secondary audience

Adults seeking help with patterns involving confidence, habits, motivation, stress, emotional eating or personal direction, provided the service and claims remain within Christine’s qualifications and scope.

### Separate B2B audience

Wellness practitioners, retailers and aligned businesses interested in Australian Aromatic Essences, wholesale or reseller arrangements.

### Who the homepage should not try to serve simultaneously

- Consumers shopping for a low-cost single product
- People comparing clinical mental-health treatment providers
- Start-ups wanting a conventional business consultant
- Wholesale/reseller prospects
- Every person interested in generic wellness

These audiences can have dedicated landing pages, but should not collectively dictate the homepage.

- Warm but not effusive

- Excessive capital letters
- “Miraculous results”
- “Permanent change” and guaranteed outcomes
- “Revolutionary” unless demonstrably justified
- “Quantum” where it has no clearly explained meaning
- Repeated three-word slogans without explanation

- Feel clear again
- Understand what is keeping you stuck
- Change patterns that no longer serve you
- Personal and professional clarity
- Experienced, one-to-one guidance
- A practical next step
- Work at a pace that fits your life
- In person and online across Australia

## 10. Visual design direction

### Desired impression

Contemporary, refined, warm, intelligent, feminine without being delicate, and premium without looking like a luxury fashion brand.

### Recommended palette

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary ink | Deep Aubergine | `#2E2030` |
| Secondary | Muted Plum | `#675064` |
| Accent | Aged Rose | `#B88178` |
| Warm highlight | Soft Champagne | `#C9A66B` |
| Background | Porcelain | `#F7F3EE` |
| Alternate background | Dusty Blush | `#EDE2DF` |
| Text | Charcoal | `#292629` |
| White | Warm White | `#FFFCF8` |

Champagne should be used sparingly for rules, icons or small details—not gold gradients or large blocks.

### Typography

Recommended premium pairing:

- **Headings:** Cormorant Garamond or Fraunces
- **Body/UI:** Manrope or DM Sans

If licensing allows, a more distinctive editorial serif may replace the Google-font heading. Use no more than two type families. Body copy must remain highly readable on mobile.

### Photography

Commission a small brand shoot rather than building the site around generic stock photography.

Shot list:

- Hero portrait with negative space
- Seated conversational portrait
- Working/listening detail
- Environmental images at a consultation setting
- Natural close-up portraits
- Product still life for essences
- A few wide images suitable for page banners

Art direction: warm daylight, tactile neutral materials, plum/aubergine accents, mature confidence, real expressions. Avoid over-retouching and spiritual-wellness clichés.

### Layout system

- Generous whitespace
- Editorial typography
- Maximum content width around 1200–1280px
- Body copy measure around 65–75 characters
- Strong hierarchy and short sections
- Alternating warm-neutral backgrounds
- Restrained rounded corners; avoid making every section a card
- Subtle motion only: fades, small image reveals and hover transitions
- No auto-rotating sliders, parallax overload or decorative animation

### Accessibility

- WCAG 2.2 AA target
- Minimum 16px body type, preferably 18px for editorial copy
- Strong colour contrast
- Visible keyboard focus states
- Proper labels and error messages on forms
- Alt text based on image purpose
- Respect reduced-motion settings
- Tap targets at least 44px

## 12. SEO and content migration

### Priority SEO themes

Final keywords require proper volume and competition research, but useful intent clusters include:

- personal transformation coach Melbourne
- life and business coach Melbourne
- business mentor for women Melbourne
- leadership clarity coach
- mindset coach Melbourne
- hypnotherapy Melbourne / Surrey Hills / Box Hill / Frankston, only where accurate and commercially prioritised
- online personal transformation coaching Australia
- Australian aromatic essences and relevant product-specific searches

Do not force the entire site to rank for “wellness.” It is broad, competitive and insufficiently connected to the main conversion.

### On-page rules

- One clear search intent and one H1 per page
- Unique title and meta description
- Plain-language introduction near the top
- Descriptive internal links
- FAQs based on real buyer questions
- Author/reviewer and updated dates for wellbeing content
- No suburb-page multiplication unless Christine genuinely services those locations and pages contain useful local information

### Technical migration requirements

1. Crawl and export every current indexable URL before development.
2. Record traffic, rankings, backlinks and conversions by landing page.
3. Decide keep, improve, consolidate or remove for each URL.
4. Map every changed URL to its closest relevant replacement with a 301 redirect.
5. Preserve valuable content and links; do not redirect everything to the homepage.
6. Remove template demo links, placeholder content, thin archives and accidental indexation.
7. Generate XML sitemaps and submit them through Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
8. Configure canonical URLs, robots rules, Open Graph data and image metadata.
9. Verify GA4, Search Console and any advertising pixels with consent controls.
10. Monitor 404s, indexing, rankings and conversions for at least 8–12 weeks after launch.

### Structured data

Implement only markup supported by visible content:

- `Organization` or suitable `LocalBusiness` subtype after business classification is confirmed
- `Person` for Christine
- `WebSite`
- `BreadcrumbList`
- `Service` on service pages
- `Product` and `Offer` on genuine product pages
- `Article` or `BlogPosting` on insights
- `FAQPage` only when FAQs are visible and eligible

Do not expect self-serving testimonial/review markup on the organisation’s own pages to produce star snippets.

## 13. Technical build brief for the AI agent

### Build objective

Create a fast, accessible, mobile-first, premium website that makes A New You’s primary offer immediately understandable and supports separate service and product funnels.

### Platform principles

- Use a modern component-based framework and a manageable CMS for pages, articles, testimonials, programs and products.
- If WooCommerce is retained, rebuild the theme and simplify categories rather than migrating legacy page-builder code wholesale.
- If using a headless or custom AI-built stack, use a mature commerce/checkout provider; do not custom-build payment security.
- Give the client simple editing controls without allowing arbitrary layout breakage.

### Required reusable components

- Header and mobile navigation
- Hero variants
- Trust/credential strip
- Problem/outcome cards
- Service pathway cards
- Process steps
- Founder profile feature
- Testimonial block and story grid
- Program feature and program template
- Article cards and article template
- FAQ accordion
- Product grid and product detail components
- Booking/contact form
- Final CTA band
- Full legal/footer system

### Content model

Programs should store: name, short promise, audience, status, format, duration, inclusions, investment, start/availability, FAQs and CTA.  
Testimonials should store: quote, name/initial, role/context, pathway, consent status and optional image.  
Articles should store: title, excerpt, author, reviewer where needed, publish/update dates, topic, hero image, body, FAQs and related service.  
Products should use the commerce system as the source of truth for price, stock, variants and fulfilment.

### Performance targets

- Aim for Core Web Vitals “good” thresholds on representative mobile pages
- Serve responsive AVIF/WebP images
- Avoid loading video until requested
- Self-host/subset fonts where licensing permits
- Minimise third-party scripts
- Use server rendering or static generation for public content
- No layout shift from images, fonts or consent banners

### Security and privacy

- TLS everywhere
- Spam protection that does not create unnecessary accessibility barriers
- Least-privilege admin access and multifactor authentication
- Automated backups and tested restore process
- Privacy-conscious analytics and a genuine consent mechanism where required
- Do not collect health details in a generic contact form
- Secure booking platform with clear data handling
- Keep plugins/dependencies minimal and maintained

### AI implementation instruction

The AI builder must not invent:

- Qualifications
- Professional memberships
- Client results or testimonials
- Program inclusions, dates or pricing
- Product ingredients or therapeutic claims
- Clinic addresses
- Awards, media coverage or bestseller status

Use clearly labelled placeholders for unconfirmed facts and produce a final content-verification checklist before launch.

## 18. Non-negotiable redesign principles

1. One clear primary audience on the homepage.
2. One dominant promise and conversion action.
3. Problems and outcomes before proprietary terminology.
4. Services, programs and products organised as distinct pathways.
5. Christine’s experience converted into structured proof.
6. Fewer pages, with each page doing a defined job.
7. Calm, premium visual authority rather than visual or verbal excess.
8. No invented credentials, results or claims from the AI builder.
9. Full URL migration and redirect plan.
10. Mobile accessibility, performance and conversion treated as core design requirements.

## 19. Final recommendation

A New You should not be redesigned as a prettier version of the current website. That would preserve the confusion.

The redesign should make a strategic choice: **Christine is the trusted guide; transformation is the primary offer; services and programs are the pathways; products are a supportive but separate commercial stream.**

The new site should feel like meeting an experienced practitioner who listens carefully, identifies what matters and offers a clear next step. It should replace information overload with confidence, sequence and restraint.

If that is achieved, the site will finally communicate the real asset the business already has: Christine’s depth of experience and her ability to help capable people move forward when surface-level answers have not been enough.
